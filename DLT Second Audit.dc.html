Method, and what changed since the first audit

The previous audit found four screens of hardcoded fixtures. That is no longer the product. There is now a real data layer — dlt-store.js, 2,176 lines — holding users, trips, seats, holds, bookings, passengers, payments, provider events, refunds, passes, boarding events, waitlist, reviews, notifications, sessions and an audit trail, with optimistic concurrency, idempotency keys, role permissions and a policy engine. The screens read from it and cannot bypass it. A real QR encoder produces genuine ISO 18004 symbols. A {{ suiteOriginal }}-assertion rule suite (store-tests.html) passes end to end. Most of the first audit's P1 list is genuinely closed.

So this audit is not a repeat. It assumes nothing from the last one and re-tests the current behaviour: I read every implementation file, walked the student and returning-student journeys, worked the admin console as an operator, and then attacked the store directly in the running page to see what the rule suite does not cover. Four defects were reproduced live rather than inferred — they are marked reproduced below, with the observed output.

What I could not verify

Three things are outside what this environment can prove, and I will not claim them either way. The 3D frame itself: the WebGL buffer cannot be read back for capture here, so the journey's cinematography is audited from its source — the splines, the speed profile, the rig table and the scene graph — not from pixels. Judgements about camera variety and stops follow from the authored numbers; judgements about beauty do not appear at all. The QR symbols: the encoder is a correct-looking implementation and the app decodes its own codes, but no physical scanner or third-party reader was put in front of one. Real devices: layout was inspected at width breakpoints, not on a handset.

Device limits, stated plainly. No camera and no physical scanner exist in this environment, so what is proved is the decode path — a rendered pass decoded back to its exact token by the same library the console runs — and the failure paths, exercised by stubbing the camera to refuse permission and to report no device. Camera capture on real handsets, and third-party hardware scanners, remain untested by anyone but a person holding a phone. jsQR loads from a pinned CDN (jsqr@1.4.0) with no subresource integrity hash, because the bytes cannot be fetched here to compute one; vendor the file and add the hash before launch.

NOT READY

Not because it is unfinished-looking — it is the opposite, and that is the risk. It reads as a shipped product while a seat can be sold twice, a free booking can refund ₹259, a fare change strands a booking forever, a waitlist can be joined but never claimed, and every rule the specification assigns to a server is enforced in a file the student can edit. Close the six P0s and the fourteen P1s and this becomes 🟡 READY WITH FIXES; it does not become 🟢 until the store is a server.
